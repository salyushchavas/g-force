# G-Force visual, content and media review

23 October 2026. Follow-up: the user requested the client row in the hero, then
selected the earlier transparent single-colour marks and removed its Pause
button. The 21 prepared wordmarks now fill that space without white cards;
the latest direction keeps scrolling automatic through interaction. This supersedes the earlier client-row
omission in the body of this review. [Current evidence](client-hero/browser-results.json).

Reviewed the rendered site, all 15 routes at 1440×900 and
390×900, individual media files and sampled film sequences. Scores are design
judgements out of 10, based on relevance, hierarchy, composition and usability;
they are not test results or a claim of universal perfection.

**Current assessment: approximately 9/10 for the interface, 8.7/10 for media
selection.** The largest defects were subject relevance, repeated video controls
and the opening's dependence on scrolling. More effects would not improve the
support or course-selection experience. The current pass makes the opening
purposeful and replaces the two weakest service images.

## What changed

- Cloud and backup now shows actual storage drives and activity indicators in a
  server rack. Weather footage is retained only where it provides atmosphere.
- Cybersecurity now uses a security-key/laptop still: a recognisable account and
  device security subject. The empty office-window film is no longer selected.
  The career article uses the same relevant asset instead of network cabling.
- Short service films play once and rest. There is no persistent Pause toolbar
  or looping background workload. Reduced motion/data-saving visitors choose
  Play explicitly; failed playback retains a poster and a recovery action.
- The opening presents the complete original name, moves upward automatically
  on a fresh load and hands over to a readable message with both audience
  actions. Visitors can interrupt it. The final composition is useful without
  the sculpture.
- The accessibility statement now describes the actual short-clip/Play
  behaviour, rather than promising a Pause button that was removed.

## Reference comparison and the path beyond 9

The reference contributes a photographic dawn, clear sculptural depth and
purposeful spatial movement. G-Force deliberately retains its own original
letterforms, crimson actions, upright Archivo and service/learning content. The
result is visually related; it is not a claim to reproduce Air pixel for pixel.
The pale glass could still gain more distinctive highlight/refraction modelling;
that should be measured against rendering cost rather than adding continuous
motion throughout content.

The highest-value remaining improvement is real, permissioned G-Force material:
instructors and labs, the actual team at work, confirmed course schedules/fees,
and substantiated customer outcomes. These would make the site more distinctive
and credible than replacing adequate stock with different stock. Completing the
real form/booking integration and verifying final contact/hours also improves
production usefulness. Those inputs cannot honestly be fabricated to award 10/10.

## Evidence and limits

The [30-case route inventory](design/route-visual-inventory.json) records all 15
routes at desktop/mobile widths: no JavaScript page errors, no horizontal
overflow, no broken **displayed** images and no unintentionally hidden main
headings in the stable rendered scan. The desktop-only training starter panel is
intentionally hidden at 390px and its lazy image is not requested. This scan used
reduced motion for stable section inspection; normal-motion opening, interruptions,
playback, mobile decoding and performance need their separate regression evidence.
The report does not turn a static screenshot pass into a motion test.

The updated end-hero compositions are recorded at [desktop](design/hero-end-1440.jpg)
and [mobile](design/hero-end-390.jpg). A separate [security crop check](design/security-crop-check.json)
verified the final service image and article cover at 320, 390 and 1440px: each
image decoded, the service had no video element, and no page errors occurred.
The security key remains visible in the [desktop portrait crop](design/security-service-1440.jpg)
and the [mobile crop](design/security-service-390.jpg). The motion regression
records the actual moving intro and final compressed-player checks. Physical-phone/field
measurements and client sign-off remain distinct from automated browser checks.

New source/licensing details are preserved in [the security-media credits](../../MEDIA_LICENSES-security.md)
and [the clip pipeline](../../scripts/video-clip-sources.json). The security-key
AVIF is approximately 2.5KB at 640px and 10KB at 1920px, replacing a video request
